End-to-end AIRB process for PCORI

A six-stage structured review lifecycle with an agentic AI agent handling pre-screening so the human board focuses only on cases that need real deliberation. No standing meetings required for standard cases.

1
Submission — Staff submits intake form
Any PCORI staff member submits the AI Initiative Intake Form via a web portal (Microsoft Forms / SharePoint). Submission triggers an automated acknowledgment email and opens a tracked case in the AI pipeline dashboard within 1 hour.
2
Agentic Pre-Screen — AI agent runs 7 automated checks
Within 24 hours, an AI agent validates the submission against PCORI's defined rule set: data sensitivity classification, patient privacy flags, vendor risk lookup, funding influence detection, bias risk assessment, mission alignment scoring, and human-in-the-loop verification. Agent produces a Pre-Screen Report with a composite risk rating and routing recommendation.
3
Triage Gate — CIO reviews pre-screen report (2 business days)
The CIO (or designee) reviews the agent's Pre-Screen Report and routes the case: Auto-Approve (low risk, all fast-track conditions met), Standard Board Review (medium risk), Full Board + Legal Review (high risk), or Return to Submitter for clarification.
4
Board Review — AIRB members deliberate asynchronously (5 business days)
Board members receive the Pre-Screen Report and intake form via email/Teams. Each member submits their review assessment using a structured scoring template covering mission alignment, data risk, bias risk, and implementation feasibility. No standing meeting required for standard cases — async review only. High Risk cases require a live session.
5
Decision — Agent aggregates scores, CIO ratifies, decision letter issued
The AI agent aggregates board member scores, applies weighted voting rules, and produces a decision recommendation (Approve / Approve with Conditions / Return for Clarification / Reject). The CIO ratifies the recommendation. A formal decision letter is sent to the submitter within 10 business days of original submission.
6
Monitoring — AI agent tracks post-approval compliance (ongoing)
For approved initiatives, the agent sends quarterly check-in prompts to the initiative owner, tracks disclosure registration compliance, and flags any scope drift for board review. Annual re-certification required for all active AI tools. Incidents trigger immediate escalation to board chair and CIO.
Key metrics
Target cycle time
10 business days from submission to decision for standard reviews. 3 days for expedited low-risk cases that meet all fast-track conditions.
Expected volume
Estimated 3–8 AI initiative requests per quarter at PCORI's organizational scale. Process is deliberately low-overhead to match this volume.
Human touchpoints
CIO triage gate (2 days) plus async board scoring (5 days). No standing meetings required unless a High Risk case requires live deliberation.

Agentic Review Engine

The AI agent runs a structured, rule-based pre-screening pass on every submission before it reaches the board. This reduces board burden and ensures consistent, bias-free application of PCORI's AI governance rules every time.

What the agent does — and what it does not do
The agent applies defined rules, scores submissions, flags risks, and recommends routing. It does NOT make final decisions — all approvals require human ratification by the CIO or board. The agent is a structured triage tool, not a decision-maker. Its Pre-Screen Report is transparent and auditable.

Automated checks performed — in sequence

Step 1 — Auto · Completeness check
Verifies all required fields are populated and that no high-risk fields are marked "Unknown." If mandatory fields are empty or ambiguous, agent sends a clarification request to the submitter within 24 hours and pauses the review clock until resolved.
Step 2 — Auto · Data sensitivity classifier
Classifies the data types involved using the submitter's responses. PHI selection triggers an immediate High Risk flag. PII of grantees or applicants triggers an Enhanced Review flag. Internal operational data only (no personal data) is classified as Low Risk. Classification is applied to all downstream routing logic.
Step 3 — Auto · Human-in-the-loop check
If the submission indicates AI makes fully automated decisions with no human override capability, agent immediately flags as High Risk and escalates to mandatory full board review regardless of any other scores. There is no auto-approval path for fully automated AI decision-making at PCORI.
Step 4 — Auto · Mission alignment score
Agent parses the mission alignment narrative and scores it 0–10 against PCORI's five National Priorities for Health using keyword and semantic matching. Scores below 3 trigger a clarification request. The score is included in the Pre-Screen Report and weighted in the composite risk rating.
Step 5 — Auto · Vendor risk lookup
If a vendor is named, agent checks against PCORI's approved vendor registry. Known approved vendors (e.g. Microsoft within the existing M365 tenant) pass automatically. Unknown or unvetted vendors trigger a vendor due diligence flag, pausing the review until IT Security completes a vendor assessment.
Step 6 — Auto · Funding influence check
If the submitter indicated any direct or indirect connection to PCORI funding decisions, grant review, priority-setting, or grantee outcomes, agent assigns a Funding Integrity Risk flag. This flag makes General Counsel participation mandatory in the board review phase.
Step 7 — Auto · Bias risk assessment
Agent reviews the bias risk narrative field and cross-references it against the data types, intended user population, and workflow description. If the tool touches patient populations or research community data without explicit bias mitigation documentation, agent adds a Bias Review flag and recommends the AI Ethics / Bias Expert be invited as an ad hoc board member.
Final — Gate · Composite risk rating & routing recommendation
Agent computes a composite risk score (Low / Medium / High) from all seven checks and produces the Pre-Screen Report. The report includes: composite risk rating, all flags raised, recommended routing path (Auto-Approve / Standard Board Review / Full Board + Legal), suggested questions for board members to consider, and a summary of the submission for CIO triage.
Handoff — Human · CIO triage gate (within 2 business days)
The CIO receives the Pre-Screen Report and either ratifies the agent's routing recommendation or overrides it with documented justification. The CIO can fast-track a Low Risk case to auto-approval, escalate a Medium Risk case to full board review, or return the submission to the submitter for additional information.
Post-approval monitoring (ongoing)
Quarterly check-in
Agent sends a structured 5-question check-in to the designated initiative owner every 90 days. Responses are logged in the AI pipeline dashboard. Any indication of scope drift triggers a board notification and potential re-review.
Annual re-certification
All approved AI tools must complete re-certification annually. Agent generates a simplified re-review package pre-populated with prior approval data. Full board review is only required if scope, data access, or vendor has changed materially.
Incident escalation
If an AI-related incident is reported — bias complaint, data breach, unexpected harmful output, or media inquiry — the agent immediately escalates to the board chair and CIO for emergency review within 24 hours.
Disclosure tracking
Agent tracks whether approved tools have been added to PCORI's public AI disclosure register. Sends automated reminders at day 20 post-approval. Escalates to CIO if disclosure is not completed within the 30-day SLA.

AIRB Board Composition

A lean 5-person standing board, sized for PCORI's scale and estimated request volume of 3–8 cases per quarter. Board reviews are primarily asynchronous — no standing monthly meeting is required unless a High Risk case requires live deliberation.

Chair
CIO / Chief Information Officer
PCORI's internal CIO
Holds the triage gate authority and final ratification authority for all decisions. Casts the deciding vote in tied cases. Serves as primary accountability holder for AI governance at PCORI. Chairs any live deliberation sessions required for High Risk cases. Receives the monthly AI pipeline dashboard.
Standing Member
General Counsel / Legal Representative
PCORI's internal legal counsel
Reviews legal permissibility, vendor contract AI clauses, regulatory compliance (ACA, HIPAA where applicable), and conflict-of-interest implications. Required reviewer — non-optional — for any initiative touching funding decisions, grantee data, or external-facing AI outputs.
Standing Member
Chief Officer, Program Operations
Carolyn Best, Ph.D. (or designee)
Evaluates mission alignment with PCORI's five National Priorities for Health and operational impact on program delivery. Assesses whether AI use in research-adjacent workflows is consistent with PCORI's patient-centered research standards, stakeholder commitments, and Board-level transparency expectations.
Standing Member
IT Security / Privacy Officer
PCORI's IT Security lead
Reviews data security architecture, vendor data handling agreements, encryption standards, and access control design. Assesses data residency and sovereignty risks. Required reviewer — non-optional — for any tool that sends data outside PCORI's Microsoft 365 tenant or processes PHI or PII.
Rotating Member — 12-month term
Division Director (rotating annually)
Rotates across PCORI's divisions on a 12-month appointment cycle
Provides the operational end-user perspective and ensures AI governance decisions are grounded in practical PCORI workflows. The rotating seat structure ensures all PCORI divisions eventually have direct AIRB experience, building enterprise-wide AI governance literacy and cross-divisional buy-in over time.
Ad hoc subject matter experts — non-voting, invited as needed
Patient / Stakeholder Advocate
Invited for any initiative that could affect PCORI's research community, patient populations, or public-facing outputs. Directly aligned with PCORI's foundational patient-engagement philosophy and its Congressional mandate.
AI Ethics / Bias Expert
Invited for High Risk cases involving patient data, research-adjacent decisions, or known algorithmic bias risk in the AI model. Can be drawn from PCORI's existing research grant network or external advisory relationships.
Bravent Senior AI Advisor
The Senior AI Strategy & Implementation Advisor quality-reviews the agent's Pre-Screen Report before CIO triage and provides technical AI governance expertise as a non-voting resource for board deliberations.
Voting rules
Quorum, majority, and dissent documentation
Standard cases require 3 of 5 board members to submit scores (quorum). Decisions are majority-based (3+ votes). High Risk cases require all 5 members plus a live synchronous session before scoring. The CIO holds a tie-breaking vote in 2-2 split decisions. Dissenting opinions are documented in the official decision record — consistent with best practice for transparent, accountable AI governance and PCORI's open governance culture.

Decision Rules — AIRB Ruleset

These rules are hardcoded into the agentic review engine and applied consistently to every submission. The ruleset is reviewed annually by the full board and updated as PCORI's AI maturity and regulatory landscape evolves.

Auto-reject triggers

No board review pathway exists for submissions that meet any of these conditions.

✕
Fully automated decisions affecting funding or research outcomes
Any AI system that makes autonomous funding award, denial, or research priority decisions without human oversight is automatically rejected. PCORI's Congressional mandate and mission integrity require human accountability for all consequential decisions related to research funding.
✕
PHI processed by a vendor without a HIPAA BAA
Any initiative that sends protected health information to a vendor that has not executed a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with PCORI is automatically rejected. No exceptions. The BAA must be in place before any submission involving PHI can be accepted for review.
✕
No designated human initiative owner
Every approved AI initiative must have a named, accountable human owner responsible for ongoing compliance, quarterly check-ins, annual re-certification, and disclosure registration. Submissions without a designated owner are returned to the submitter before any agent pre-screening begins.

High Risk triggers

Mandatory full board review plus live synchronous session required. Cannot be fast-tracked.

!
Patient or grantee data involved
Any AI tool that accesses, processes, or generates outputs based on patient health data, PCORnet research data, or individual grantee personal information triggers a mandatory full board review. The Patient/Stakeholder Advocate is invited as an ad hoc non-voting member.
!
Enterprise-wide or external-facing deployment
Any initiative intended for all PCORI staff, or for external stakeholders including grantees, research partners, or the public, triggers a mandatory full board review due to the broad organizational risk exposure and potential reputational implications for PCORI's mission.
!
Direct or indirect influence on funding or research decisions
Any AI tool with direct or indirect influence on PCORI grant review, priority-setting, award decisions, or grantee outcome assessments triggers a mandatory full board review. General Counsel's participation is required and non-optional in this scenario.
!
Unknown, unapproved, or unvetted vendor
Any vendor not on PCORI's approved technology vendor list triggers an IT Security vendor due diligence requirement before board review can begin. The review clock pauses until vendor assessment is completed and a risk determination is issued by the IT Security Officer.

Fast-track / auto-approval conditions

All five conditions must be true simultaneously for a submission to qualify for CIO fast-track approval without full board review.

✓
Approved vendor operating within PCORI's existing Microsoft tenant
Tool is a known Microsoft product — Copilot, Azure OpenAI, Power Automate — operating fully within PCORI's existing Microsoft 365 tenant with no new external data connections or integrations.
✓
Internal PCORI operational data only — no PHI, PII, or sensitive research data
The tool only accesses PCORI's own internal operational content: staff documents, emails, meeting notes, or calendar data. No patient data, grantee personal data, sensitive research data, or PCORnet data is involved.
✓
Human makes all final decisions — AI is purely assistive
AI is used exclusively as a drafting, summarizing, or productivity assistance tool. All AI outputs are reviewed and approved by a human before any action, communication, or decision is taken. No AI output is acted upon without human review.
✓
Individual or team-level deployment only — no enterprise or external rollout
Use is scoped to a single staff member or a specific internal team. No enterprise-wide deployment and no external-facing use is planned in the current scope of the submission.
✓
No connection to funding decisions, research outcomes, or grantee assessments
The tool has no direct or indirect connection to PCORI's grant review processes, research priority-setting, award decisions, or any outputs that could influence grantee relationships or research community assessments.

Decision Outcomes & SLAs

Every AIRB review results in one of four formal outcomes. SLAs are tracked by the AI pipeline dashboard and reported monthly to the CIO and quarterly to PCORI's leadership team.

Approved
Initiative may proceed as submitted. Disclosure registration in PCORI's public AI register required within 30 days. Quarterly monitoring check-ins begin immediately. Annual re-certification date is set at approval.
Approved with Conditions
Initiative may proceed only after specified conditions are met. Examples: vendor BAA must be executed, bias mitigation plan must be documented, scope must be limited to named teams only. Conditions are tracked by the agent with 30-day SLA per condition. Failure to satisfy conditions within 60 days results in automatic suspension.
Returned for Clarification
Submission requires additional information before review can proceed. The review clock pauses. Submitter has 10 business days to provide the requested information before the case is closed. Re-submission opens a new case at no penalty.
Rejected
Initiative does not meet PCORI's AI governance standards as currently designed. Decision letter includes the specific reasons for rejection and, where applicable, a clear description of what changes would be required for a future resubmission to be considered. Rejection reasons are tracked for policy improvement.

Governance → Execution Bridge

Every AIRB decision is converted into a structured execution plan with defined ownership, timelines, and measurable outcomes. This ensures governance is not just advisory — it directly drives operational delivery and accountability across PCORI. This layer is the core of Section 3.1.

Why this layer exists
Most AI governance processes stop at approval. PCORI's AIRB goes further: every decision — approve, approve with conditions, or reject — produces a structured execution record that the CIO can track, report on, and hold teams accountable to. Governance without execution follow-through is just paperwork.
AI Initiative Prioritization Matrix

All submitted initiatives are scored across five dimensions to ensure consistent, defensible prioritization across the AI portfolio. The composite score determines pipeline sequencing and resource allocation.

Scoring dimensions — each rated 1 (low) to 5 (high)

AI Execution Scorecards — reported monthly to CIO

Four scorecard dimensions provide the CIO with a complete picture of portfolio health — covering delivery progress, compliance posture, business value, and program maturity.

Execution Progress
Tracks delivery status across the initiative lifecycle:
  • Lifecycle stage: Planned → In Progress → Pilot → Scaled → Closed
  • Milestone completion rate vs planned schedule
  • Dependency and blocker tracking
  • Condition closure rate for approved-with-conditions cases
  • Overall on-track / at-risk / delayed status per initiative
Risk & Compliance Status
Ongoing governance visibility across all active initiatives:
  • Privacy and data compliance status
  • Bias monitoring indicators and flag resolution
  • Disclosure registration completion vs 30-day SLA
  • Audit readiness rating
  • Open risk flags and remediation owner
Business Impact Metrics
Measures whether approved initiatives are delivering stated value:
  • Productivity gains vs baseline (e.g. time saved per workflow)
  • AI idea-to-decision cycle time reduction
  • Tool adoption rate by target user population
  • User satisfaction scores from quarterly check-ins
  • Copilot utilization analytics (active users, feature usage)
Governance Health
Tracks AI program maturity and governance posture over time:
  • % of active initiatives currently compliant with all conditions
  • Condition closure rate (approved-with-conditions cases)
  • Annual re-certification completion rate
  • Open risk flags across the portfolio
  • AI portfolio maturity score (quarterly assessment)
