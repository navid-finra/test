Copilot Pilot Closeout Assessment

A structured formal closeout of PCORI's Copilot pilot program. This is not a simple usage report — it is a governance-grade assessment that produces the evidence base for the enterprise rollout decision and informs PCORI's Board-level reporting on AI maturity.

What a closeout assessment is — and why it matters
A pilot closeout converts raw pilot experience into structured, auditable findings. It answers three questions the CIO and Board need before approving enterprise rollout: Did Copilot deliver measurable value? Are the risks understood and manageable? Is PCORI organizationally ready to scale?
Key questions the assessment must answer
Utilization
Did pilot participants actually use Copilot, and how consistently?
Look at active user rates vs. enabled user rates. A pilot where only 30% of licensed users were active tells a different story than one where 80% used it weekly. Segment by role and division to find where adoption was strong vs. weak.
Value Delivery
Did users experience measurable time savings or quality improvements?
Benchmark: Microsoft telemetry shows 7–10 minutes saved per accepted Copilot output. For PCORI, measure against pre-pilot baselines for common tasks: meeting summarization, document drafting, email synthesis. Ask users directly via structured survey.
Use Case Fit
Which specific workflows generated the most value, and which generated confusion or errors?
The best Copilot use cases in a research-funding context are: summarizing long documents, drafting communications, synthesizing meeting notes, and generating first drafts of reports. Grant review and funding-related workflows need special scrutiny — flag any AI-assisted use in these areas.
Risk Realization
Did any risk scenarios materialize during the pilot?
Check for: oversharing incidents (Copilot accessing content users didn't expect it to), output accuracy concerns, inappropriate use in grant-related workflows, staff privacy concerns about their content being indexed. These must be documented and addressed before rollout.
Organizational Readiness
Is PCORI's data environment clean enough for enterprise Copilot?
Copilot surfaces everything a user has access to through Microsoft Graph. If SharePoint permissions are poorly managed, Copilot will expose content broadly. A permissions audit is a prerequisite for enterprise rollout — not optional. Assess SharePoint hygiene, Teams structure, and file classification coverage.
Change Readiness
Are staff ready to adopt Copilot as a standard part of their workflows?
Measure confidence levels, training adequacy, manager support, and champion network strength from the pilot. Identify resistance patterns by division or seniority. Staff who feel AI threatens their role need targeted communication before enterprise rollout, not after.

Adoption Metrics & Usage Analytics

What data to collect, what it means, how to track it, and what benchmarks to compare against. Metrics are organized by the three phases Microsoft recommends: baseline (pre-deployment), 90-day activation, and 6-month outcome measurement.

The most common measurement mistake — don't make it
89.6% of organizations track usage (seat counts, prompt volumes). Usage metrics alone are insufficient and misleading. Seats purchased ≠ seats active ≠ value delivered. PCORI should measure business process improvements and organizational outcomes — not just whether staff opened Copilot. This is the standard Microsoft itself now recommends.

How to track it — the PCORI measurement stack
M365 Admin Center
Built-in Copilot usage report. Access via: Admin Center → Reports → Usage → Microsoft 365 Copilot. Available at 7, 30, 90, 180-day windows. Export to CSV. Data available within 72 hours. No additional license needed.
Viva Insights (Power BI)
Advanced Copilot adoption report with role-level segmentation. Requires Viva Insights Analyst role + Power BI Desktop. Up to 13 months of historical data. Runs as a scheduled query. Requires Viva Insights license.
Copilot Dashboard
Microsoft's recommended tool for IT leaders. Shows readiness, adoption, and impact by team. Available in Viva Insights. Provides power-user identification and feature-level adoption breakdown. Recommended for CIO reporting.
User Survey (structured)
Microsoft Forms or SharePoint survey deployed at 30, 60, 90, and 180 days post-activation. Captures satisfaction, time savings, use cases, and responsible use confidence. Required — telemetry alone is insufficient.
Diary Study (optional but powerful)
Pilot participants log each Copilot use for one week: task type, time saved, output quality, issues encountered. UK Government's DBT pilot used this model successfully with a 32% response rate. Produces the richest qualitative data.
IT Support Ticket Log
ServiceNow or equivalent IT ticketing system. Tag all Copilot-related tickets. Track volume, resolution time, and issue category over time. Leading indicator of adoption friction and training gaps. Existing system — no new tooling needed.

Enterprise Rollout Framework

A phased, conditions-based rollout plan for Copilot across PCORI. The framework is designed to protect PCORI's data environment, build staff confidence, and generate documented evidence of value at each phase before proceeding to the next.

Critical prerequisite before any enterprise rollout
Copilot accesses everything a user can access through Microsoft Graph — emails, SharePoint documents, Teams messages, calendar data. If SharePoint permissions are poorly managed (which is common), Copilot will expose content users shouldn't see. A SharePoint permissions audit and data classification review must be completed and signed off by the IT Security Officer before Phase 1 licenses are issued beyond the pilot group.
Phased rollout — four stages
Phase 0
Pre-launch foundations
SharePoint permissions audit. Data classification. Acceptable use policy published. Role-based training built. AIRB approval obtained.
→
Phase 1
Power user cohort
20–30 high-readiness staff across 2–3 divisions. Champion network seeded. 60-day intensive measurement period.
→
Phase 2
Division-by-division rollout
Expand to full divisions in order of readiness score. Training mandatory before activation. 90-day assessment gate.
→
Phase 3
Enterprise-wide + Copilot Studio
All eligible staff activated. Custom agents built in Copilot Studio. Agentic AI layer introduced per architecture in Tab 4.

Rollout prerequisites — gate conditions between phases
Gate: Phase 0 → Phase 1
  • SharePoint permissions audit complete and remediated
  • Data classification labels applied to sensitive SharePoint libraries
  • AI Acceptable Use Policy published and acknowledged by all pilot cohort
  • AIRB approval obtained (or reconfirmed for expanded scope)
  • Role-based training curriculum built and tested
  • IT Support team trained as Copilot help desk
Gate: Phase 1 → Phase 2
  • Phase 1 active user rate > 60%
  • Phase 1 user satisfaction score > 65%
  • No unresolved security incidents from Phase 1
  • Training completion 100% for Phase 1 cohort
  • Responsible use compliance 100% for Phase 1
  • Phase 1 learnings documented and incorporated into training
Gate: Phase 2 → Phase 3
  • Enterprise-wide active user rate > 55%
  • Data environment assessed as clean by IT Security Officer
  • Copilot Studio governance framework in place before first agent deployment
  • Agentic AI policy approved by AIRB (separate submission required)
  • Board-level briefing on AI portfolio delivered
  • Annual re-certification process operational
Hard stop conditions — rollout pauses
  • Any confirmed oversharing incident involving grant-related or research data
  • User satisfaction below 50% in any division after 60 days
  • Discovery of Copilot being used in grant review workflows without AIRB approval
  • Unresolved data classification gaps in a division before that division activates

Agentic AI Architecture for PCORI

How to build purposeful AI agents on top of the Copilot platform, how agents should interact with each other and with external systems, and what guardrails must be in place before any agent is deployed at PCORI.

What agentic AI means for PCORI specifically
An AI agent is a system that can take actions — not just generate text. At PCORI, agents should start simple: document intelligence agents that summarize and route, workflow automation agents that move data between systems, and knowledge agents that answer staff questions from internal documents. Fully autonomous agents that take consequential actions (send communications, make decisions) should not be deployed without explicit AIRB approval and human-in-the-loop design.
Recommended PCORI agent architecture — four layers
1
Foundation Layer — Copilot + Microsoft Graph
All PCORI agents are built on top of the Microsoft Copilot platform and Microsoft Graph. This gives agents access to PCORI's M365 data (emails, documents, Teams, SharePoint) through a governed, permission-aware interface. No agent should access data outside of what the underlying user or service account is explicitly permitted to access. This is the security boundary — Microsoft Graph enforces it.
Microsoft 365 Copilot Microsoft Graph API SharePoint / Teams / Exchange Azure Active Directory / Entra ID
2
Agent Development Layer — Copilot Studio + Azure AI Foundry
PCORI's custom agents are built using Copilot Studio (no-code/low-code, accessible to non-developers) or Azure AI Foundry (full code, for IT-built agents). Every agent must be registered in the PCORI AI inventory, assigned an Entra ID identity (so its actions are attributable and auditable), and approved by the AIRB before deployment. Copilot Studio agents are visible in the M365 Admin Center; Foundry agents require separate logging setup.
Copilot Studio (no-code agents) Azure AI Foundry (code-first agents) Agent Identity via Entra ID Model Context Protocol (MCP)
3
Agent Interaction Layer — How agents talk to each other and to external systems
When PCORI deploys multiple agents (e.g. an AIRB intake agent + a document summarization agent + a training agent), they may need to hand off tasks to each other. Use Microsoft's Agent-to-Agent (A2A) Protocol for inter-agent communication. Each agent-to-agent handoff must be logged. Agents should use the principle of least privilege — they only call other agents or external systems that are explicitly permitted in their AIRB-approved scope. External system integrations (e.g. SharePoint, HR systems, grant management platforms) require separate data connection approvals.
Agent-to-Agent (A2A) Protocol Model Context Protocol (MCP) Azure Logic Apps (workflow orchestration) Power Automate (low-code integration)
4
Governance & Observability Layer — Microsoft Purview + Defender + Log Analytics
Every agent interaction must be logged, auditable, and monitored. Use Azure Monitor and Application Insights for agent telemetry. Microsoft Purview for data governance and DLP enforcement. Microsoft Defender for Cloud for security alerts on agent behavior. Establish a central agent control plane — a dashboard visible to IT and the CIO — that shows all deployed agents, their identity, their data access scope, their last action, and any security alerts. No agent should be invisible to the governance layer.
Microsoft Purview (data governance + DLP) Microsoft Defender for Cloud Azure Log Analytics Application Insights (telemetry) Foundry Observability
How PCORI staff should use agents — usage model
Staff-facing agents (approved use)
Staff interact with agents through Copilot Chat, Teams, or a custom interface. Agents answer questions, summarize documents, draft content, and route tasks. Staff always see the agent's output before any action is taken — human-in-the-loop is mandatory for all staff-facing agents in Phase 3.
Background / workflow agents (restricted)
Agents that run without direct user interaction (e.g. auto-routing documents, monitoring for compliance triggers). These require higher AIRB scrutiny and must have defined scope, clear action limits, and a human notification mechanism for any consequential action taken.
Autonomous decision agents (prohibited without AIRB)
Agents that make consequential decisions without human review — sending external communications, modifying grant records, approving requests — are prohibited at PCORI without a separate, specific AIRB approval. These represent the highest risk class.
Guardrails — non-negotiable for every PCORI agent
🪪
Every agent has a registered identity (Entra ID)
Agents are assigned a service principal in Microsoft Entra ID with explicit, scoped permissions. This means every agent action is attributable, auditable, and revocable. An agent without an identity cannot be monitored or controlled. This is Microsoft's own security standard for Azure AI Foundry agents as of 2025.
🔒
Least-privilege data access — agents can only see what they need
Each agent's data access scope is defined at build time and approved by the IT Security Officer. An AIRB intake summarization agent, for example, should only access the AIRB SharePoint site — not all of PCORI's SharePoint. Principle of least privilege is enforced via RBAC in Azure.
👁
All agent interactions are logged and retained for 90 days minimum
Every prompt, tool call, and output produced by a PCORI agent is logged using OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions and stored in Azure Log Analytics. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days for audit purposes. PII is redacted at capture time. Logs are accessible to the IT Security Officer and the CIO on request.
🛡
Content safety filters enabled on all agents
Azure AI Content Safety filters are applied to all agent inputs and outputs. This blocks harmful content, detects prompt injection attacks (where a malicious document tries to hijack the agent's behavior), and flags ungrounded outputs before they reach users. Microsoft's own Responsible AI Standard requires this for all Azure AI deployments.
🚫
Grant review and funding workflows are an explicit exclusion zone
No PCORI agent may participate in, inform, or process any grant application review, funding priority assessment, or award decision workflow without a specific, standalone AIRB approval with General Counsel review. This is not a default guardrail — it is a hard prohibition that must be embedded in each agent's system prompt and enforced at the policy layer.
👤
Every agent identifies itself as AI to users
Any agent interacting with PCORI staff or external parties must identify itself as an AI system at the start of every interaction. This is required by Microsoft's Responsible AI Standard, recommended by NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, and is becoming a regulatory expectation under the EU AI Act. The agent must never impersonate a human.
⏸
Defined scope limits and shutdown authority
Every agent has a documented scope boundary — what it can and cannot do. The IT Security Officer and CIO hold shutdown authority for any deployed agent. Incident response procedures (including how to take an agent offline and notify affected users) are documented and practiced before the agent goes live. No agent is "owned" only by the team that built it.

Integration architecture principles for PCORI
1. Build on Microsoft Graph — don't bypass it
All PCORI AI integrations should access internal data through Microsoft Graph, not through direct database connections or file system access. Graph enforces user permissions, provides audit logs, and is the security-approved path. Building outside Graph creates data exposure risks and governance gaps that are difficult to remediate later.
2. RAG before fine-tuning
For PCORI's use cases, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — where the AI retrieves relevant PCORI documents and grounds its answers in them — is the right pattern. Fine-tuning Azure OpenAI models on PCORI data is expensive, requires careful data governance, and is not needed for the use cases PCORI will encounter in the near term. Use Azure AI Search + Azure OpenAI for RAG.
3. Copilot Studio before Azure AI Foundry
Build first agents in Copilot Studio (no-code, M365-integrated, governed out of the box). Graduate to Azure AI Foundry only when Copilot Studio's capabilities are genuinely insufficient for the use case. Foundry requires more IT development capacity, more governance setup, and introduces more complexity. The maturity model is: Copilot → Copilot Studio agents → Foundry agents.
4. One agent, one job, one owner
Each PCORI agent should have a single, clearly defined purpose. Do not build multi-purpose agents that attempt to handle multiple unrelated workflows — they are harder to govern, harder to audit, and when they fail, the failure mode is ambiguous. Assign a named human owner to every deployed agent who is accountable for its behavior and re-certification.

Responsible AI & Disclosure Standards

Procedures, standards, and disclosure requirements for PCORI's AI program — grounded in Microsoft's Responsible AI Standard, NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, and PCORI's own culture of transparency and patient-centered accountability.

Why responsible AI standards matter especially for PCORI
PCORI holds a unique position: it funds research that evaluates AI in healthcare while also deploying AI internally. It cannot hold grantees to rigorous transparency standards while operating its own AI program opaquely. PCORI's responsible AI standards must be consistent with — and ideally exemplary of — the standards it expects from the research it funds. This is both a governance requirement and a reputational imperative.
PCORI's responsible AI principles — the operating foundation
⚖️
Fairness
AI systems at PCORI must not produce biased outputs that disadvantage any patient population, disease area, or research community
🔍
Transparency
PCORI discloses its AI use publicly and ensures staff understand when and how AI is being used in their workflows
👤
Human Oversight
A human is accountable for every consequential AI-assisted output. AI augments; humans decide.
🔒
Privacy
AI systems handle patient data, grantee data, and staff data with the highest privacy standards PCORI applies to any data type
📋
Accountability
Every AI system at PCORI has a named owner. Every decision made with AI assistance is traceable. The AIRB provides the accountability structure.
🛡
Safety
AI systems do not take actions that could harm PCORI's mission, its grantees, its research integrity, or the patients whose outcomes PCORI's work ultimately serves
📐
Reliability
AI outputs are tested, monitored, and validated. Known limitations are documented and communicated to users. Hallucinations are treated as a known risk, not an edge case.
🌱
Inclusiveness
AI adoption at PCORI does not disadvantage staff who are less tech-confident. Training, support, and change management are provided equitably across all roles and experience levels.
Procedures — how PCORI operationalizes responsible AI
1
Pre-deployment: AIRB review for all AI initiatives
Every new AI tool or use case goes through the AIRB process (Section 3.1) before deployment. The AIRB pre-screen evaluates fairness risk, data sensitivity, human oversight design, and mission alignment. No AI tool is deployed at PCORI without a named accountable owner and a documented responsible AI assessment.
2
During deployment: Bias monitoring and output quality checks
For any AI system touching research-adjacent or stakeholder-facing content, PCORI implements structured output quality checks: a sample of AI-generated outputs is reviewed monthly by a human reviewer for accuracy, bias, and appropriateness. For Copilot, this means periodic spot-checks on AI-assisted documents in sensitive workflow areas. Issues are escalated to the AIRB.
3
Incident response: What to do when AI goes wrong
PCORI maintains a documented AI incident response procedure. Incidents include: AI-generated content that is factually wrong and was not caught before use, bias complaints related to AI outputs, data exposure through AI systems, and AI being used in prohibited workflows (e.g. grant review). Incidents are reported to the AIRB chair and IT Security Officer within 24 hours. The AIRB reviews and may suspend the tool pending investigation.
4
Ongoing: Annual re-certification and maturity assessment
Every active AI tool is re-certified annually through a streamlined AIRB review. The annual re-certification checks whether the tool's data access, vendor status, scope, and usage have changed materially since approval. The quarterly AI portfolio maturity assessment benchmarks PCORI's overall AI governance posture against the Microsoft Maturity Model for M365 and the NIST AI RMF.
Disclosure standards — what PCORI discloses, where, and when
Public AI Disclosure Register
PCORI maintains a publicly accessible register of all approved and active AI tools. Published on PCORI's website. Updated within 30 days of any new approval. Lists: tool name, vendor, purpose, data types accessed, deployment scope, approval date, and initiative owner. This is consistent with PCORI's open governance culture and its Board-level transparency standards.
External documents / publications
Any PCORI-published document, report, funding announcement, or research standard that was materially drafted or edited using AI assistance carries a disclosure statement. Suggested language: "This document was prepared with the assistance of AI tools. All content has been reviewed and approved by PCORI staff." This applies to web content, Board materials, stakeholder communications, and published research standards.
Stakeholder communications
When PCORI communicates with grantees, applicants, or research partners using AI-assisted drafts, the communication need not individually disclose AI assistance — but PCORI's publicly published AI use policy must be readily accessible so stakeholders can understand how AI is used in PCORI's operations.
Internal staff disclosure
PCORI staff are informed at onboarding and through training which AI tools are approved for use at PCORI, what the permitted use cases are, what is prohibited, and what the Acceptable Use Policy requires. Staff are notified of any new AI tool approvals within 30 days via internal communications.
Board-level reporting
The PCORI Board of Governors receives a quarterly AI portfolio briefing covering: active AI tools, key decisions made during the quarter, any incidents, compliance posture, and the portfolio maturity score. This is consistent with PCORI's open forum governance model and the Board's existing oversight role.
Agent identification requirement
Every PCORI AI agent that interacts with staff or external parties must identify itself as an AI system at the start of each interaction. Agents must never impersonate a human staff member. This applies to Copilot Studio agents, Teams bots, and any automated communication system built on Azure AI. Required under Microsoft's Responsible AI Standard and aligned with emerging regulatory expectations.
Acceptable use policy — key provisions
Permitted uses of Copilot and AI tools at PCORI
  • Drafting internal documents, emails, and reports (with human review before use)
  • Summarizing meeting notes and long documents
  • Synthesizing publicly available research information
  • Generating first drafts of communications (not final without human review)
  • Analyzing non-sensitive operational data
  • Supporting IT tasks: code review, documentation, troubleshooting
Prohibited uses — explicit exclusions
  • Any participation in grant application review or funding decisions
  • Processing of patient health information (PHI) without explicit AIRB approval
  • Making autonomous decisions on behalf of PCORI without human review
  • Sending external communications without human review and approval
  • Using unapproved AI tools not on the PCORI AI disclosure register
  • Inputting confidential grantee data into any AI tool outside PCORI's M365 tenant
Alignment to external frameworks
PCORI's responsible AI standards are designed to align with: NIST AI Risk Management Framework (Govern → Map → Measure → Manage); Microsoft Responsible AI Standard (the standard Microsoft's own engineers follow, and that governs all Azure AI services PCORI uses); and the emerging EU AI Act requirements for transparency and human oversight — which, while not legally binding on PCORI, represent international best practice that PCORI's research community increasingly expects of organizations in the healthcare AI space.

Tooling setup — step-by-step for IT
Step 1 — Enable M365 Admin Center Copilot report
  1. Sign in to Microsoft 365 Admin Center as Global Admin or Reports Reader
  2. Navigate to Reports → Usage → Microsoft 365 Copilot
  3. Select the Usage tab to view adoption and usage metrics
  4. Set time window to 90 days for pilot analysis
  5. Select Export (CSV) from the ellipsis menu for raw data
  6. Schedule a weekly export and store in a SharePoint folder accessible to the Advisor
Step 2 — Configure Viva Insights Copilot Adoption report
  1. Assign Insights Analyst role in Microsoft 365 Admin Center to designated IT staff and Advisor
  2. Open Viva Insights analyst experience → Create analysis → Templates
  3. Select Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption template
  4. Set time period to Last 3 months; enable Auto-refresh
  5. Add org attributes: Division, Role Level (for segmentation by cohort)
  6. Connect to Power BI Desktop; publish to Power BI Service for CIO access
Step 3 — Enable Copilot Dashboard for CIO
  1. In M365 Admin Center, navigate to Reports → Copilot → Copilot Dashboard
  2. Assign CIO and Advisor the Microsoft 365 Usage Summary Reports Reader role
  3. Dashboard shows readiness, adoption, and impact by team — no additional configuration required
  4. Bookmark the Dashboard URL for CIO weekly check-in reference
  5. Set up a recurring Power BI export for the monthly executive dashboard
Step 4 — Deploy closeout survey in Microsoft Forms
  1. Advisor creates survey in Microsoft Forms (PCORI tenant)
  2. Key question areas: usage frequency, time savings estimate, best use cases, worst experiences, confidence level, training adequacy, concerns
  3. Set to anonymous responses for honest feedback
  4. Distribute via email and Teams message to all pilot participants
  5. Leave open for 10 business days; send one reminder at day 5
  6. Export responses to Excel; Advisor analyzes and includes in Closeout Report

Enforcement, ROI & Risk Register

How guardrails are technically enforced in Azure and M365 (not just defined in policy), how to build the ROI case the CIO can present to the Board, and a structured risk register covering the threats that matter most for PCORI.

How guardrails are technically enforced — not just policy, but control
🔐
Microsoft Purview DLP — prevents sensitive data from entering Copilot
Configure Data Loss Prevention policies in Microsoft Purview to detect and block sensitive content types (PHI labels, grant application content labels, PII patterns) from being processed by Copilot. When a labeled document is referenced in a Copilot prompt, Purview intercepts and either blocks the response or strips the sensitive content. Policy scope: all SharePoint sites containing labeled sensitive content. IT Security Officer owns policy configuration and monthly review.
Microsoft Purview Compliance Portal DLP policy: Block Copilot access to PHI-labeled content DLP policy: Alert on grant-content Copilot prompts
🏷
Microsoft Purview Sensitivity Labels — classify and restrict content at source
Apply sensitivity labels to SharePoint libraries containing grant applications, research data, and grantee PII. Labels trigger automatic encryption and access restrictions. Copilot respects these labels — it will not surface labeled content to users who lack the corresponding permissions. This is the foundational data protection control that makes enterprise Copilot deployment safe at PCORI. Without labels, Copilot exposes everything a user can access.
Purview Information Protection Labels: Confidential-GrantData, Restricted-PHI, Internal-Only
📋
Copilot usage restrictions in M365 Admin Center — control who can use what
In M365 Admin Center, configure Copilot access policies to restrict Copilot to approved cohorts only during phased rollout. This ensures only Phase 1 approved users have active Copilot licenses. Use Azure AD security groups to manage cohort membership — adding and removing users as each phase activates. Admin Center also allows disabling specific Copilot features (e.g. web search) if PCORI's data governance requires it.
M365 Admin Center → Copilot settings Azure AD security groups per rollout phase Feature-level controls (web search toggle)
🔭
Azure Log Analytics + Application Insights — agent and AI activity logging
For Azure AI Foundry agents (Phase 3), all agent interactions — prompts, tool calls, outputs, token counts — are logged using OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions to Azure Log Analytics. Logs are retained for 90 days minimum. The IT Security Officer configures alerts for anomalous agent behavior: unusually high token consumption, repeated failed tool calls, or access to out-of-scope data. PII is redacted at capture time using Purview's built-in redaction capability.
Azure Log Analytics workspace OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions Microsoft Defender for Cloud AI alerts
🤖
Azure AI Content Safety — blocks harmful outputs before they reach users
For any Azure AI Foundry or Azure OpenAI deployment, Azure AI Content Safety filters are applied to both inputs and outputs. These filters detect: harmful content, prompt injection attacks (malicious documents trying to hijack agent behavior), ungrounded outputs (AI claims not supported by the retrieved documents), and jailbreak attempts. Content Safety is a mandatory component of every Azure AI deployment at PCORI — it cannot be disabled for production systems.
Azure AI Content Safety (Foundry) Groundedness detection Prompt injection protection
🚨
Microsoft Defender for Cloud — threat protection for AI workloads
Microsoft Defender for Cloud provides security alerts specific to AI workloads: unusual data access patterns by agents, suspected prompt injection attacks, and anomalous API call volumes. The IT Security Officer reviews Defender alerts weekly. Any AI-related alert classified as High severity triggers the PCORI AI incident response procedure — the AIRB chair and CIO are notified within 24 hours and the affected tool may be suspended pending investigation.

ROI model — how PCORI quantifies and reports Copilot's value
Important caveat on ROI estimates
Forrester's Microsoft-commissioned 2024 study projected 8+ hours saved per user per month and 100%+ ROI over 10 months. These are projections based on pre-current-version deployments. Microsoft's own independent telemetry data shows 7–10 minutes saved per accepted Copilot output. Acceptance rates of 20–40% are realistic for early deployment. PCORI should build its own ROI model from actual pilot data rather than relying on vendor projections. The framework below shows how to do that.
PCORI ROI calculation framework — three scenarios
ScenarioAssumptionMonthly hours saved (200 users)FTE equivalentAnnualized value (est.)
Conservative 2 hrs/user/month saved (low adoption, low acceptance rate) 400 hrs/month ~2.4 FTE ~$240K/yr at $50/hr blended rate
Moderate 5 hrs/user/month saved (healthy adoption, good use cases) 1,000 hrs/month ~6.0 FTE ~$600K/yr at $50/hr blended rate
Strong 8 hrs/user/month saved (Forrester benchmark, strong adoption) 1,600 hrs/month ~9.5 FTE ~$960K/yr at $50/hr blended rate
How to build PCORI's actual ROI figure: At 30 days post-Phase 1 activation, survey users for actual hours saved per week. Multiply by weeks in period × active users. Compare to Copilot license cost ($30/user/month). Present payback period to CIO. Update quarterly as adoption grows. This is what gets presented to the Board — not vendor projections.
PCORI-specific value metrics beyond time savings
Board memo preparation
If Copilot reduces Board memo drafting time by 50%, Executive Office saves approximately 4–6 hours per Board cycle. At 4 Board cycles per year, that's 16–24 hrs/year for a high-value task.
Meeting summarization
PCORI runs high volumes of committee meetings. Copilot-assisted meeting summaries in Teams eliminate manual note-taking. For 10 meetings/week × 30 min saved = 5 hrs/week across the organization.
Stakeholder communication drafting
Engagement & Dissemination team sends high volumes of stakeholder communications. First-draft generation with Copilot can cut drafting time by 40–60%, freeing staff for higher-value engagement work.
